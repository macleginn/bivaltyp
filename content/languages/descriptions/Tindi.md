## Grammar notes

### Basic clause structure and the transitive construction

Clause structure in Tindi is dominated by case marking on nominals and by gender-number agreement with the nominative (S/P) argument on verbs. Constituent order indicates only information structure. Bivalent transitive verbs have their patientive argument in the nominative case and their agentive argument in the ergative case. Most affective and perception verbs mark the experiencer in the affective case. Some oblique arguments use a postposition, following the genitive, the affective case, or the nominative case.

### Case system
There are twenty-six cases in Tindi: five grammatical cases and twenty-one spatial cases. 
The nominative case is unmarked, but the nominative form of nouns ends in a vowel, which may change before oblique cases. 
The ergative case is marked by *-ji* (after vowel < *-di* after consonant). 
The expression of adnominal possessors has two subtypes. When possessors are nouns denoting male referents, they usually just agree in gender/number with their head. Other nouns take a dedicated genitive marker *-ƛa*, probably related ti the interlocative case -ƛi. 
Other grammatical cases are the dative (*-ɬa*) and the affective (*-ba*).
Tindi spatial cases are based on locative morphemes of which most go back to Proto-Daghestanian (cf. Alekseev 1999). The seven locational series are multiplied by three directional series, but the essive and lative series are not distinguished segmentally: essive suffixes are stressed, while the same suffixes with lative meaning are unstressed.
