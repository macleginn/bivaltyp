## Grammar notes

### Basic clause structure and the transitive construction

Case marking displays the nominative-accusative alignment. In the basic transitive construction, the subject (A- or S-argument) is in the nominative, and objects (P-arguments) show differential marking. The accusative case is always used with personal pronouns and posession-marked nouns and typically used with proper names, human and plural-marked nouns, as in (1). Indefinite, singular, non-possessive, inanimate objects typically appear in the unmarked (nominative) form, as in (2). Otherwise, both options are usually acceptable; animate and definite nouns are more likely to stand in the accusative than inanimate or indefinite.


```

(1)  Petʼa   vitʼ-e        Maša-jez
     PN.NOM  wait-PRS.3SG  PN-ACC
     ‘Petja is waiting for Masha.’

(2)  Petʼa   kńiga     baśt-i-z
     PN.NOM  book.NOM  take-PST-3SG
     ‘Petja took a book.’

```

Verbal predicates agree with their subjects in person and number. Non-core arguments are typically flagged by oblique cases or by postpositions, which combine with unmarked or genitive-marked nouns.

Although SOV is the most frequent word order, all orders are acceptable in principle, with SVO being the second most popular option.

### Case system

Beserman has 22 cases: 8 non-spatial cases (nominative, accusative, genitive, second genitive, dative, caritive, adverbial, instrumental), 8 spatial cases (locative, lative, elative, prolative, egressive, terminative, approximative, recessive) and 6 so-called personal-local cases (‘at / to / ... someoneʼs place’), which are combinations of the suffix *-ń-* with 6 regular spatial cases. Traditionally, some of these cases have had different labels in Beserman or [Udmurt]({{ site_url_j }}/languages/descriptions/Udmurt.html): second genitive = *ablative*, locative = *inessive*, lative = *illative*.
